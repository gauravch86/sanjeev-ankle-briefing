1 Plain-English summary

MRI of the right ankle shows an infection-pattern arthritis, focused mainly on the subtalar joint (between talus and heel bone), with marrow oedema in the talus, oedema reaching the front of the calcaneum, and two subchondral cysts in the talus (about 13×11 mm and 16×10 mm).

There is loculated / septated fluid and mild synovial thickening at the subtalar joint, plus a milder “reactionary” effusion at the tibiotalar (main ankle) joint, and mild soft-tissue swelling around the foot and distal leg.

The plain X-ray a day earlier looks almost normal — bones and joint spaces look fine on film. That often means bone destruction has not yet advanced far enough to show on X-ray, so disease may still be early-to-moderate on the destruction scale, even though symptoms have lasted years.

The radiologist’s impression is: features suggestive of infective arthritis — ? tubercular, and asks for clinical correlation. In India, osteoarticular TB is an important possibility for a long-standing single joint — but MRI alone does not confirm TB.

At 115 kg, every step loads the ankle heavily; weight is a load factor and rehab partner, not a “cause” or a cure by itself.

Family understanding of the plan: sample / biopsy with cuts, then possibly surgery to clear infection and address damaged cartilage — that tissue work is how TB vs pyogenic infection is actually proved.

3 Why the X-ray can be normal

Disclaimer: Educational synthesis for family discussion with treating doctors — NOT a diagnosis, NOT a substitute for orthopaedic / infectious-disease judgment.

What X-ray said

Alignment proper; normal outline/contour/density; no lytic/sclerotic lesion; joint spaces unremarkable; soft tissue no abnormality. Essentially a normal plain film.

Why that still fits

Early osteoarticular TB and early septic arthritis often look quiet on X-ray for weeks–months while MRI already shows marrow oedema, synovitis, and effusion.

Take-home: Normal X-ray does not cancel an abnormal MRI. X-ray is less sensitive for early marrow and soft-tissue infection. MRI is doing the “early warning” job here; tissue sampling does the confirmation job.
  • X-ray strengths: alignment, big bone loss, obvious fractures.
  • MRI strengths: marrow oedema, synovitis, loculated fluid, early cartilage/subchondral change.
  • Classic pattern: chronic monoarticular pain + normal X-ray + “infective” MRI → raise TB/septic work-up, don’t stop at “arthritis of age”.

5 Why sample / cuts / surgery is being discussed

Disclaimer: Do not tell anyone to skip biopsy if doctors advise it. This section explains why surgeons ask for tissue — it is not a consent form.

For suspected osteoarticular TB, the gold standard is tissue and/or fluid sent for a full panel — not MRI alone:

  • GeneXpert / CBNAAT on tissue or pus (rapid TB DNA + rifampicin resistance clue)
  • AFB smear and mycobacterial culture (culture takes time but remains important)
  • Histopathology — granulomas, caseation, synovitis pattern
  • PLUS routine bacterial culture + Gram stain — so pyogenic septic arthritis is not missed

Aspiration alone

Needle fluid helps, but sometimes volume is low or organisms are scarce — result can be inconclusive.

Arthroscopic / open biopsy

Commonly needed for proper tissue. “Cuts” / portals let the surgeon see, sample, and often debride infected synovium or pus — diagnostic and sometimes therapeutic.

Why this matters

Wrong empiric treatment delays healing. Right sample guides ATT vs antibiotics vs both, and whether more cartilage work is needed.

Family-reported plan: sample/biopsy with cuts → then possibly further surgery for septic arthritis / infection / damaged cartilage. Confirm exact steps, risks, and consent wording with the operating surgeon.

7 Weight 115 kg — load, not blame

Disclaimer: Weight loss is an adjunct, not a cure for suspected infective arthritis. Do not delay biopsy/surgery for a diet plan.

Every step multiplies force through the ankle. At 115 kg (obesity), that load makes pain feel worse, swelling linger, and post-op rehab harder — especially if weight-bearing is restricted.

Before / around surgery

Discuss walker/crutches fit, bed-to-chair transfers, and home support. High load + non-weight-bearing needs a real plan.

Adjunct goals

Steady, doctor-approved weight reduction helps long-term joint comfort and heart/metabolic health — parallel track, not a substitute for infection work-up.

8 Timeline & red flags → urgent care

Disclaimer: Educational only. When in doubt, go to emergency / call the treating doctor.
Seek urgent care if: fever with chills, night sweats that are new/worsening, discharging sinus near the ankle, sudden inability to put foot down / walk, new numbness or foot-drop feeling, rapidly spreading redness, or severe uncontrolled pain.
  • Chronic background: years of difficulty walking, continuous pain, swelling — matches a smoldering process; still needs planned (not panicked) work-up.
  • After biopsy/surgery: watch wound discharge, fever, calf swelling (clot risk discussion with surgeons), and medication side-effects if ATT starts.
